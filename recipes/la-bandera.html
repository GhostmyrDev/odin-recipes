La Bandera

La Bandera's a dominican dish that strongly embraces the worldwide latino roots and dominican pride. Not only it is a simple recipe, it's also amazingly tasty! We'll estimate this recipe for 4 people.

Ingredients

Given the nature of La Bandera as a dish and its components (as it translates to "The Flag") it's something you gotta dedicate a decent amount of concentration to every part of it. So we'll be distributing the ingredients' list for each of its components.

White Rice

  • 5 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 4 cups of long grain rice
  • 4 tsp salt
  • 6 cups of water

Bean Stew

  • 2 cups of red or pinto beans
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil (preferably olive oil)
  • 1/4 tsp oregano
  • 1 cubanelle pepper (or 1 bell pepper if you can't find any cubanelle)
  • 1 red onion
  • 2 cloves of garlic
  • 1 cup of diced pumpkin
  • 1/2 cup tomato sauce
  • 4 sprigs of thyme
  • Some leaves from a celery stalk
  • 1/2 tsp cilantro
  • 1 tsp salt

Chicken Stew

  • 2 lb chicken

  • 1/4 tsp oregano
  • 1 red onion
  • 1/2 cup of celery

  • 1/2 tsp garlic

  • 4 tomatoes

  • 1/4 cups of green olives without seed
  • 1 cup of tomato sauce
  • Some cilantro leaves

Steps

Now we can move onto the cooking process! We'll cook each thing separately to combine a top-tier dish by the end of this recipe.

White Rice

  1. In a medium-sized cauldron, pour in 3 tbsp of your oil as well as the salt and let it heat up to medium heat.
  2. Once your caludron's reached that medium heat, add in the water.
  3. Now that the water's boiling, add in your rice and stir carefully to prevent the rice from sticking to the bottom of the cauldron.
  4. Let the water evaporate a little before placing the lid on and let it cook for around 15 minutes on low heat.
  5. After 15 minutes, remove the lid and pour in your remaining oil.
  6. 5 minutes later after the previous step, try the rice. It should be firm but soft on the inside. Otherwise cover with the lid again and let it cook for 5 more minutes on low heat.

Now! At the bottom of the cauldron once you've finished serving the rice, you'll find there's a thin layer of rice that's somewhat stuck to the bottom of the cauldron. That's concón, a crispy and flavourful layer of rice that most dominicans just scrape to eat along with the rice. It ain't burnt! it should be golden. You should definitely try it!
